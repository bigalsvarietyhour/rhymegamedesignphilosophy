design system

The Rhyme Game

Color, typography, components, screens & philosophy

read this first · the question that organizes every screen

"Is the player playing the toy, or using the tool?"

The Rhyme Game is two things in one app. The gameplay is a toy the player plays with — tactile, kinetic, immediate. Everything else is a premium music tool — sleek, atmospheric, composed. The answer to that question decides every single visual choice that follows.

Gameplay · the toy

Chunky platform blocks with hard drop shadows. Saturated rhyme colors. Fredoka 20–26px. Buttons feel like physical objects you can almost touch. Used only on the active gameplay grid and in-game CTAs.

rust
may

Premium-tool · the music app

Subtle gradients, soft glows, atmospheric depth. Glass-like surfaces with hairline borders and soft shadows. Generous breathing room. Rich gold accents. Think Spotify, Apple Music, Headspace.

Audio Lag
Volume

Never mix the two vocabularies on one screen. Keeping the playful, chunky energy exclusively inside gameplay is what makes both modes feel more like themselves. The menus feel premium because they're surrounded by restraint. The gameplay feels kinetic because the chunky energy is reserved for it.

Philosophy

Three principles guide every design decision. When in doubt, return to these.

1 · Premium tool, playful toy

Menus, settings, stats, and config screens look like a premium music app. The gameplay looks like a toy you can almost touch. Two distinct visual languages for two distinct modes — and never the two shall mix. Premium isn't sterile; the cream-and-purple palette keeps even the calmest surfaces feeling like this app.

2 · Color carries meaning

Every color has a job. Purple is the world. Bright yellow #FFC72C is the gameplay accent. Rich gold #F5C648 → #C28C00 is the premium-tool accent. Rhyme colors group sounds. Pink invites creativity in the flare zone. Never use color for decoration — if it doesn't communicate, it doesn't belong.

3 · Brand assets are not UI

The logo, the bouncing ball, the trail behind it, and any future mascots are pre-rendered artwork. They get placed onto screens as image assets — never reconstructed from theme components, never given drop shadows to feel "tappable." System UI follows the tokens; placed artwork follows its own rules. The two coexist on a screen.

Signature moves

Three patterns that make premium-tool screens feel like a real product, not a wireframe. Visual specs are in the Tokens tab.

★ The gem treatment

Hero CTAs on premium-tool screens (Free Trial, Upgrade, the play orb) use a 3-stop gold gradient with internal sheen, specular highlight, inset edge shadows, and an outer atmospheric bloom. The combined effect: a luminous polished object that looks expensive. Save it for the one important action per screen.

▤ The lower shelf

Screens with persistent bottom nav use a dark shelf at the bottom (#1F0F4F → #14093D) that holds only the navigation tabs. The play orb breaks the shelf's top edge — its upper half sits in the hero zone, its lower half in the shelf. The mini-player floats above the shelf as a separate premium surface.

◌ Premium surfaces

The replacement for flat translucent cards. A subtle gradient from rgba(184,164,255,0.06) at top to rgba(26,15,61,0.45) at bottom, plus a hairline border at rgba(255,248,220,0.10), plus a soft drop shadow. The combination reads as raised glass on deep purple.

Do & don't

do

Reach for premium-tool vocabulary on every screen except gameplay. Use the gem treatment only for genuine hero CTAs (one per screen, max). Use rich gold on premium-tool screens; bright yellow on gameplay only. Treat brand assets as placed artwork. Stack the lower shelf pattern on screens with persistent nav. Layer the play orb's bloom above other elements. Reference theme.* tokens — never hardcode hex values. Let premium surfaces breathe with generous padding.

don't

Mix gameplay and premium-tool vocabularies on the same screen. Reconstruct the logo or any brand artwork from components. Use chunky platform blocks on non-gameplay surfaces. Use bright #FFC72C and rich gold accents on the same screen. Use pure black text — always tint toward purple. Use pure white — use cream #FBF8F0. Add new brand colors. Stack multiple hero CTAs on one screen. Mix Fredoka and system font in one element.

one-line summary

The gameplay is a toy the player plays with — chunky, tactile, kinetic. Everything else is a premium music tool — sleek, atmospheric, composed. Two vocabularies, never mixed.

Typography

Two fonts, two roles. Don't mix them within a single element.

Gameplay font
Fredoka

Rhyme words, scores, button labels, screen titles, card titles. Weight 600 default, 700 for celebration. Rounded letterforms echo block radius.

System font
Inter / SF

Setting descriptions, supporting captions, paragraphs, metadata. Whatever the OS default sans is. Weight 400 body, 500 emphasis.

Spacing & shape

Border radius — 10px gameplay cells · 12px translucent cards · 14px menu buttons · 20px screen containers
Shadow offset — 4px default · 5px for primary CTA · always zero blur · no shadow on translucent cards
Grid gap — 8px horizontal · 10px vertical (extra room for shadows)
Card gap — 6–8px between translucent cards in a list
Padding — 12px 16px card internal · 20–24px container internal · 28px screen edge

Block physics (gameplay vocabulary)

Every tappable platform on a gameplay surface follows these rules. Build one component, reuse everywhere.

Anatomy

• Border radius: 10px
• Shadow: 0 4px 0 (no blur)
• Shadow color: same hue, darker
• Text: darkest stop of own family
• Press: translateY 4px, shadow off

Premium surfaces (premium-tool vocabulary)

The atmospheric glass card used on every non-gameplay screen — settings rows, stats cards, mini-player, list items. Replaces the older "flat translucent card" pattern.

Premium surface title
Supporting description in Purple 200
›
• Background: linear-gradient(180deg, rgba(184,164,255,0.06), rgba(26,15,61,0.45))
• Border: 0.5px solid rgba(255,248,220,0.10) (hairline)
• Border radius: 16–18px
• Padding: 14–18px vertical · 16–20px horizontal
• Drop shadow: 0 8px 24px rgba(26,15,61,0.20) (blurred, purple-tinted)
• Title: Fredoka 15 / 600 in cream (#FFF8DC)
• Description: system sans 11–12 / 400 in Purple 200 (#B8A4FF)
• The gradient + hairline border + soft shadow combination is the recipe — get all three right and it reads as raised glass on deep purple.

Gem treatment (hero CTAs on premium surfaces)

The luminous polished-gold treatment for hero CTAs — Free Trial, Upgrade, the play orb. One per screen, max. Save it for the action that genuinely matters.

Recipe (5 layers):
1. Linear gradient with bright honey holding through the top 55% for text readability: #F5C648 → #F5C648 → #C28C00 (stops at 0%, 55%, 100%)
2. Internal sheen overlay (top half, ~18% white, curved bottom)
3. Specular highlight (small blurred white ellipse near top, ~30% opacity)
4. Inset edge shadows: top rgba(255,255,255,0.32), bottom rgba(140,90,0,0.40)
5. Outer bloom: radial-gradient(rgba(232,168,0,0.4) → transparent) blurred 18px

• Border radius: 32px for pill CTAs, 50% for orbs
• Title font: Fredoka 22 / 700 in #3D2400
• One per screen. If three buttons use this treatment, none feel special.

Lower shelf architecture

The persistent bottom navigation pattern. Dark band holds the tabs; the play orb breaks its top edge as the signature element.

Tools
• Shelf gradient: linear-gradient(180deg, #1F0F4F, #14093D)
• Shelf padding: 18px top · 20px sides · 32px bottom (or safe-area inset)
• Contents: navigation tabs only (Stats, Tools) — minimal chrome
• Play orb: margin-top: -42px from shelf's top edge so its upper half overlaps the hero zone
• Orb's outer bloom positioned with top: -78px to spill upward over the mini-player
• z-index layering: mini-player (1) → shelf (2) → orb bloom (20) → orb (21)
• If a mini-player is present, it floats above the shelf as a separate premium surface, casting its own drop shadow onto the dark zone.
